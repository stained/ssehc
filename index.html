Chess with the board moving instead of the pieces. You command a colour of square, not a colour of piece — and any piece standing on one of your squares is yours to move.

    Rules
    1. White moves white squares; Black moves black squares. A square belongs to whoever's colour it is — not to the piece standing on it.
    2. A square moves the way its piece moves. A square with no piece cannot move at all. Pawns step one forward or diagonally forward; knights jump; bishops, rooks and queens slide any distance along their lines.
    3. Moving swaps the pieces. A long move swaps them one square at a time, so a slider pushes its way through an occupied board.
    4. Every square the path enters is painted your colour. A rook or queen square can repaint seven cells in one move.
    5. Paint the whole board your colour to win. With no movable square you pass; if neither side can move it is a draw.

    Only 32 pieces stand on 64 squares, so half the board is immobile but perfectly paintable. And because the pieces swap along every path they migrate — you will often find yourself moving the other side's pieces, and losing your own.
